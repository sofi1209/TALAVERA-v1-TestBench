# TALAVERA v1 schematic audit

9 November 2026 · Bench and operator station · Revision A · GitHub main at `43e980364137857c72099dcaf6f4be3cf3cbf186`

**Disposition — hold schematic release.** The current design has faults that prevent the bench from powering normally, prevent the station from obtaining its documented supply through the harness, and leave the firing output without a return. The interlock and continuity defects would remain after those connectivity issues were addressed. The hardware-only control architecture is worth keeping. The primary measurement path can be simplified and validated separately from the safety and telemetry circuits.

## What was checked

- Reviewed both uploaded schematic PDFs, their KiCad 10.0.5 netlist exports and ERC reports, and the handoff. Cloned GitHub and inspected both committed KiCad schematics, their generators, requirements, safety decisions, fabrication lists, firmware and bring-up plan. The committed PCB files contain outlines without footprints or routing.
- Re-extracted pin connectivity from the committed schematics with the repository extractor pointed at the symbols embedded in each sheet. All non-power-symbol pin groups matched the attachments, including unnamed groups and no-connect pins. Bench **59 components, 76 groups**. Station **41 components, 48 groups**. Re-ran the project validator with the embedded symbols and reproduced **0 errors / 1 warning** on bench and **0 / 0** on station. The stock tool has a Windows symbol path and skips unresolved symbols, so a bare invocation elsewhere can give misleading results.
- The supplied KiCad ERC reports show **1 error / 2 warnings** on bench and **0 / 0** on station. Local KiCad 9.0.2 could not open the committed files, so I could not independently rerun native KiCad 10 ERC. The reported bench findings agree with the connectivity. I enumerated all 16 static ARM/FIRE/E-STOP/plug combinations and several single faults in a software model of ideal relay contacts. That test assumes healthy supplies, omits transients and is independent of the diode error. No board, firmware build, hardware waveform, physical harness or live test was available.
- **Verified** identifies a source, schematic, netlist or model result. **Conditional** identifies a consequence dependent on a stated assumption, physical test, part selection or revised circuit. Component values without verified part-level evidence remain unqualified.

## Polarity and contact sweep

- **Series Schottky** — D_RP1 is reversed. K is on `+12V_FUSED` and A on `+12V_PROT`.
- **Bench flybacks** — D_K1 and D_K3 are K to `ARM_LINE`, A to GND. D_K2 is K to `FIRE_LINE`, A to GND. D_K4 is K to `+12V_PROT`, A to `ESTOP_LOOP`. Each diode is reverse-biased while its coil is normally energized. Verify physical polarity and contact-release timing with the suppression circuit fitted.
- **Station flyback** — D_BUZZ1 is K to `+12V_STN`, A to `BUZZ_DRV`. This orientation is consistent with a low-side inductive load. The specified sounder and drive are separately incorrect. The Q_BUZZ divider computes 4.86 V at a 12 V ARM line versus the onsemi device's ±20 V absolute gate rating. Match symbol G/S/D to physical pads 1/2/3. [onsemi NTS4001N](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/nts4001n-d.pdf).
- **LEDs** — Bench D_ARM1, D_FIRE1, D_PWR1 and D_LOG1 have cathodes on GND and resistor-fed anodes. Station D_ARMED1 and D_PWR1 follow the same pattern. D_CONT1 has its anode fed through a resistor from `+12V_STN` and cathode on the PC817 collector signal. The lamp polarity is correct for active-low signaling. Its meaning is wrong because the input circuit does not measure continuity.
- **Optocouplers** — PC817 uses pins 1/2 for LED A/K, 3 for emitter to GND and 4 for the sense collector. Both 6N137 symbols use LED A/K on 2/3 and output-side GND/OUT/EN/VCC on 5/6/7/8. Their LEDs have the intended polarity. Active inversion and drive current remain issues. [Vishay 6N137](https://www.vishay.com/docs/84732/6n137.pdf).
- **TVS and capacitors** — D_ESD1/2 are bidirectional ESD9B5.0ST5G between USB D+/D− and GNDREF, so there is no diode orientation error. The two SOD-923 packages are difficult to replace by hand. All 11 capacitor symbols across both boards are generic, unpolarized `Device:C`. Bench C1/C2 each show pin 1 on the positive rail and pin 2 on ground. Any polarized implementation needs an exact MPN, polarized symbol and verified footprint. [onsemi ESD9B](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/esd9b-d.pdf).
- **Relay rest states** — K1, K2 and K4 use COM 11 and NO 14. K3 uses COM 11 and NC 12. Ideal de-energized contacts open the three series paths and close the shunt. This verifies the drawing's contact selection. Exact Songle physical pin mapping, bounce, pickup/dropout, welds and recovery remain open. [Songle SRD listing](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/Relays_SRD-12VDC-SL-C_C30431.html).

## Power and measurement checks

**Rail chain.** On the bench, `+12V_RAW → F1 → +12V_FUSED → D_RP1 → +12V_PROT`. K4 switches `+12V_MASTER`, while F2 separately feeds `+12V_LOGGER` upstream of K4. `+3V3_LOGGER` runs the DevKitC, ADS1220, SD, SP3485 and bridge. The DNP `+5V_AMP` serves only the DNP amplifier. On the station, `J_12V1 → +12V_STN → U_5VB1 → +5V_BENCH → U_3V3B1 → +3V3_BENCH`. The fitted station optocoupler output and USB-UART are fed by `+5V_USB`, independently of the station's bench-side supply. Add the bench logger-rail PWR_FLAG only after documenting the actual source through F2. The station PWR_FLAG cannot supply an omitted harness feed.

**Budget.** For the handoff's assumed 12.0 V at regulator input and 150 mA, bench regulator dissipation is about `(12.0 − 3.3) × 0.15 = 1.305 W`, excluding regulator ground current. At assumed 55 °C ambient and 60 °C/W, the estimate is 133 °C. Actual copper, enclosure and current are unknown. A 12→5→3.3 linear cascade at the same current splits the same heat into about 1.05 W and 0.255 W. It reduces the second stage's heating but leaves the first stage dissipating about 1 W. The cited AMS1117 test range for the 3.3 V fixed part begins at 4.75 V. A nominal 5 V upstream rail therefore leaves about 0.25 V to that point before tolerance, ripple and wiring loss. Budget average and peak currents separately. Include DevKitC startup, SD writes, bus loading, bridge current `V_EXC/R_bridge`, indicators, relay coil pickup, cable drops and all normal power combinations. At nominal coil voltage the 400 Ω Songle coil is about 30 mA, or 120 mA for four coils supplied across the two domains, excluding all other loads. These values do not constitute a complete supply or fault budget. [AMS1117 data sheet](https://www.datasheets.com/advanced-monolithic-systems/ams1117/datasheet.pdf), [Songle SRD listing](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/Relays_SRD-12VDC-SL-C_C30431.html).

**Battery readback.** With an unloaded divider and 12 V at `+12V_PROT`, expected `VBAT_SENSE` is 2.105 V. The divider output impedance is approximately 8.25 kΩ. Include meter input loading, offset and ground difference in the calibration. A threshold specified at the battery terminals cannot be transferred uncorrected to a post-diode measurement.

**ADC.** With `AVDD = 3.3 V` and `AVSS = 0 V`, the enabled PGA's common mode must satisfy both `VCM ≥ max(0.825 V, 0.2 V + G·|VIN|max/2)` and `VCM ≤ 3.1 V − G·|VIN|max/2`, in addition to the differential full-scale limit set by reference voltage and gain. A balanced bridge near mid-excitation is plausible. The actual load cell can only be qualified with its offset, span, excitation and overload data. The proposed external reference is physically connected to board-side excitation, so cable drops to a four-wire bridge are outside the sense points. The placeholder 20 SPS yields a 50 ms sample interval, longer than the repository's assumed 10–30 ms rise. [TI ADS1220 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1220.pdf).

## Changes that make this bench easier to repair

1. **Partition it by fault domain.** Give the mechanical control/output circuit, logger/analog rail and station telemetry independently identifiable connectors, protection and accessible disconnects. Preserve the logger's upstream supply intent. A dead logger, SD card, USB interface or optional monitor should be diagnosable without disturbing the hardware interlocks.
2. **Make state and power visible.** Provide labeled probe points with adjacent return points for raw/fused/protected power, master, logger and analog rails, station supply, isolated USB supply, control lines, and the two ADC inputs and reference pins. Keep sensitive output test access recessed or shrouded. Put meter-friendly pin numbers and polarity on both mating drawings and the silkscreen. Document expected voltage and continuity for each troubleshooting state.
3. **Prioritize easy replacement.** Use keyed, locking, mechanically supported harness connectors, replaceable fuse holders, and clearance to extract the DevKitC and service the SD card without removing the board. Use larger hand-rework packages where space permits, especially at external connectors. Prefer a simple authorized population drawing over multiple overlapping DNP options. Validate connector, relay, fuse, USB, MOSFET and SD footprints with actual parts before layout release.
4. **Give each test a pass condition.** First re-export with the same KiCad version, require zero native ERC errors, disposition each warning and compare exports with committed sources. Add assertions for diode polarity, both endpoints of each intended conductor, rail source reachability, safety contact state and isolation boundaries. Then, under the project's written authorization, use a current-limited bench supply and inert fixtures for power sequencing, control-fault behavior, capture throughput, and full-harness USB/DAQ isolation. Measure rails and stop latency. Verify saved files after brownouts and abrupt interruption, and run a calibrated bridge-input test before relying on thrust data.

**Safety finding.** The schematic gives the MCU no direct path to drive a relay coil or the firing output. Its RS-485 signals share the same field harness and the logger shares supply and ground infrastructure. A proof that a single logger fault cannot energize the output requires analysis of the installed wiring, rail faults, clamp faults, contact faults and cross-shorts. The contact model and ERC do not establish that property. This audit gives no hardware or live-test sign-off.
