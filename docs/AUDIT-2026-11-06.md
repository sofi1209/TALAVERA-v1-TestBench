# TALAVERA v1 — Pre-fabrication audit, 6 November 2026

**Scope:** both KiCad schematics, the resolved netlist, the preliminary parts list, and the logger
firmware. **Method:** netlist extracted and resolved independently of KiCad
(`hardware/tools/netlist.py`), then each net inspected by hand.

**Status of the audited revision: NOT FIT TO BUILD.** Five findings would prevent the board from
working or would destroy parts on first power-up.

## Critical — would prevent operation or destroy parts

### C-1 · No integrated circuit on the bench board has a power supply **V**

The 3.3 V rail exists as **two unconnected nets**:

| Net | Members |
|---|---|
| `+3V3_LOGGER` | `U_3V3.2(VO)` — the regulator output — plus every decoupling cap |
| `+3V3` | `MCU1.2(VDD)`, `U_ADC1.12(AVDD)`, `U_ADC1.13(DVDD)`, `CARD1.4(VDD)`, `U_485.8(VCC)` |

Nothing joins them. **The ESP32, the ADS1220, the microSD socket and the RS-485 transceiver are all
unpowered**, while the regulator drives only capacitors.

Cause: power symbols were used for the IC supply pins (creating net `+3V3`) and net labels for the rail
(`+3V3_LOGGER`). This is the identical mistake as the earlier `GND_RTN` / `GND` split, which I fixed on
the ground net and did not then check for on the supply rails.

### C-2 · The load cell has no excitation **V**

```
LC_EXC_P   J_LC1.1(Pin_1), U_ADC1.9(REFP0)
LC_EXC_N   J_LC1.4(Pin_4), U_ADC1.8(REFN0)
```

Both excitation nets connect **only** the load-cell connector and the ADC's reference pins. No supply
drives either. The bridge is unpowered, so it produces no output and the ratiometric reference is
floating. **There is no measurement path at all** — which is the entire purpose of the bench.

### C-3 · All four relay flyback diodes are reversed **V**

| Diode | Anode on | Cathode on | Coil high side |
|---|---|---|---|
| `D_K1` | `ARM_LINE` | `GND` | `ARM_LINE` |
| `D_K2` | `FIRE_LINE` | `GND` | `FIRE_LINE` |
| `D_K3` | `ARM_LINE` | `GND` | `ARM_LINE` |
| `D_K4` | `+12V_PROT` | `ESTOP_LOOP` | `+12V_PROT` |

A flyback diode must be **reverse**-biased in normal operation: cathode to the coil's positive side.
Every one of these is forward-biased from the supply straight to the return, so each is a **direct short
across its own coil**. The relay cannot pull in, and the diode or the fuse fails.

This includes **K3, the shunt relay** — the part that holds the igniter shorted. Its safe state happens
to be the de-energised one, so the failure is not dangerous, but the bench would be permanently stuck
disarmed and the fault would look like a wiring problem in the field.

### C-4 · The buzzer flyback diode is reversed too **V**

```
+12V_STN   D_BUZZ1.2(A)      <- anode on the positive rail
BUZZ_DRV   D_BUZZ1.1(K), LS1.2(-), Q_BUZZ1.D(D)
```

Same error. When `Q_BUZZ` turns on, `BUZZ_DRV` is pulled to ground and the diode conducts **+12 V
straight to ground through the MOSFET**. `NTS4001NT1G` is a 270 mA part; it would not survive.

### C-5 · The buzzer MOSFET gate is driven at 12 V **A**

`ARM_LINE` (at +12 V when armed) connects directly to `Q_BUZZ1.G`. Small-signal MOSFETs in this class
commonly specify V<sub>GS(max)</sub> of ±8 V or ±20 V — **this must be checked against the NTS4001NT1G
datasheet before the part is used this way.**

Supporting evidence that it is wrong: IGNIS-v5 drives the *same part* from a 3.3 V microcontroller pin,
not from a 12 V rail. A gate divider or a different device is needed.

## High — wrong behaviour in a safety-adjacent indication

### H-1 · The continuity lamp indicates the opposite of the truth **V**

Tracing the loop: the bench optocoupler `U_CONT` has its collector on `PYRO_CONT_SENSE` and its emitter
on `GND`. At the station, `R_CONT_PU` (4k7) pulls `PYRO_CONT_SENSE` up to +12 V, and the lamp chain
`R_CONT_L → CONT_LAMP → R_D_CONT → D_CONT → GND` lights when that node is **high**.

So when the e-match is intact, its current lights the opto LED, the transistor pulls the node **low**,
and the lamp goes **dark**. When the match is open or absent, the node floats high and the lamp
**lights**.

**Lamp dark = good match. Lamp lit = open circuit.** That is the inverse of every operator's
expectation, and the operations manual tells them to "confirm the continuity lamp indicates a connected
match" before arming.

### H-2 · The continuity test button forces a false "good" **V**

```
CONT_BTN          J_CONTSW1.1, J_CONT_RTN1.1
PYRO_CONT_SENSE   J_CONT_RTN1.2, J_UMB1.4, R_CONT_L1.1, R_CONT_PU1.2
```

`J_CONTSW` pin 2 is on `GND`. Pressing the button therefore shorts `PYRO_CONT_SENSE` to ground — which,
per H-1, is the *lamp-dark* state, read by the operator as "continuity good".

**Pressing the test button always reports a good match, whether or not one is connected.** A test that
cannot fail is worse than no test, because the procedure instructs the operator to rely on it.

Note also that the sense current comes entirely from the bench side (`R_CONT` from `PYRO_MATCH_P`
through the opto LED), so continuity sensing is **always live** and no button is needed to initiate it.
The button has no correct function as drawn.

### H-3 · The USB ESD devices are drawn as plain rectifiers **V**

`D_ESD1` and `D_ESD2` use `Device:D`, with the anode on the data line and the cathode on `GNDREF`. As
drawn, each is a forward-biased diode from signal to ground that would clamp the USB data lines at
roughly 0.7 V and break enumeration.

The real part (`ESD9B5.0ST5G`) is a bidirectional TVS, so the physical board would work — but the
schematic does not say so, and whoever builds or reviews it is reading the schematic. Use
`Device:D_TVS`, which exists in the installed library.

## Medium — thermal, representation and margin

### M-1 · The bench 3.3 V regulator is over its thermal limit **V (computed)**

`U_3V3` is an AMS1117-3.3 in SOT-223 fed directly from 12 V. Dropping 8.7 V:

| Load | Dissipation | ΔT at 60 °C/W | T<sub>j</sub> at 55 °C ambient |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 mA | 0.70 W | 42 °C | 97 °C |
| **150 mA** | **1.30 W** | **78 °C** | **133 °C** |
| 250 mA | 2.17 W | 130 °C | 186 °C |

AMS1117 maximum junction temperature is typically 125 °C. An ESP32 with radios off plus a microSD card
writing will sit around 150 mA, so **the part runs past its limit at the assumed 55 °C ambient**.

Two supporting points. The IGNIS-v5 BOM already carries this exact concern on the same family:
*"Kept for V5. Large copper + thermal vias; thermal test decides V6."* And the **station board already
cascades 12 → 5 → 3.3**, while the bench does not — the two boards disagree with each other.

Options: cascade the bench the same way (moves most of the heat into the 5 V device but does not reduce
the total), or put a cheap switching pre-regulator at 12 → 5 and keep an LDO for the final 3.3 V so the
analog front end still sees LDO-grade ripple rejection.

### M-2 · The schematic draws a bare module but the build uses a DevKitC **V**

`MCU1` is `RF_Module:ESP32-WROOM-32D`, a 38-pin bare module. The physical part is a socketed
**ESP32-DevKitC V4**, which already carries its own 3.3 V LDO, its own EN pull-up and capacitor, its own
USB-UART bridge and its own buttons, and which exposes a *different* pin set.

Consequences visible in the netlist:

- `R_EN1` and `C_EN1` duplicate circuitry already on the DevKitC.
- Nine module pins are drawn (`SENSOR_VP/VN`, `SD0`–`SD3`, `SCK/CLK`, `SCS/CMD`, `NC`) that **do not
  exist on the DevKitC header**.
- The DevKitC is powered through its `5V`/`VIN` or `3V3` header pin, not through the module's `VDD`.
  So C-1's missing supply connection is not even to the right pin.

This was logged as a footprint question (O-16). The netlist shows it is a **schematic correctness**
question: the sheet does not represent the circuit that will be built.

### M-3 · 6N137 LED current has no margin **V (computed)**

`R_ISO1` = 330 Ω driven from the SP3485's `RO` output at 3.3 V. With an assumed LED forward voltage of
1.5 V:

- 330 Ω → **5.45 mA**
- 220 Ω → 8.18 mA

6N137 data sheets typically specify a minimum forward current around 5 mA for guaranteed switching.
5.45 mA leaves essentially no margin for CTR ageing, temperature, or the transceiver's output drop.
220 Ω is the safer choice. **Confirm against the datasheet (O-6).**

### M-4 · Roughly sixteen pins are intentionally unconnected and carry no no-connect flags **V**

The ESP32 flash pins, `K2`'s unused NC contact, the CH340's modem-control lines and the 6N137 `NC` pins
are all deliberately open. Gate 5 requires every remaining ERC item to be listed and justified, so each
needs an explicit no-connect symbol rather than being left to be re-triaged at every run.

## What this audit did not cover

- **Layout and DRC** — no layout exists yet (Gate 4).
- **The pyro and analog component values** — still blocked on O-1 and O-2, so there is nothing to check.
- **The firmware against hardware** — the ADS1220 register values are placeholders (O-2) and the code has
  never been compiled. Reviewing it now would mostly re-state what its own header already admits.
- **Whether the ADS1220 register intent is correct** — needs the datasheet, which this audit did not open.

---

## Recommendation

Do not progress to layout. C-1, C-2 and C-3 are each sufficient on their own to make the board
non-functional, and C-4 and C-5 destroy parts. H-1 and H-2 together mean the pre-arming check an
operator is told to trust cannot fail, which is the most serious finding in safety terms even though it
is not the most dramatic electrically.

All of C-1 through C-4, H-3 and M-3 are mechanical corrections with a single right answer. H-1, H-2,
M-1 and M-2 need a design decision first.
